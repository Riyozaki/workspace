# Brainstorm, before implementation or benchmark results

## Environment and scope

CPU-only sandbox, 2 logical CPUs, approximately 4 GiB RAM; no preinstalled numerical stack or accelerator. The request permits improving an existing approach. A narrowly scoped, mathematically exact optimization is preferable to an unverified claim about arbitrary neural networks. Gaussian-process (GP) regression is an AI/ML model; fitting its posterior counts as training. No claim of a new discovery will be made for established Kronecker identities.

The following estimates are **CALCULATED / HYPOTHESIZED**, not measured.

| # | Distinct idea and hypothesis | Estimated end-to-end gain / mathematics | Risks and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exact tensor spectral GP: a separable kernel on a complete Cartesian training grid permits Kronecker eigendecomposition. | Dense Cholesky costs N^3/3; spectral fitting costs sum(n_i^3) + O(N sum n_i), N=product(n_i). For 128×64, leading arithmetic reduction is thousands-fold; startup / I/O will cap wall-clock speed. Exact variance prediction can also exploit tensor contractions. Hypothesis: >10× including process startup for sufficiently large N. | Requires Cartesian training inputs, separable kernel and scalar observation noise. Finite precision, eigenvalue cancellation, variance evaluation, unfavorable small sizes. Existing technique, not a novel discovery. |
| 2 | Masked Kronecker PCG: retain tensor matvecs when training points are missing; use a full-grid inverse as a restricted preconditioner. | Per iteration O(N sum n_i) versus dense O(M^2); at k iterations compare O(k N sum n_i) with O(M^3). If k<100 and M≈N, potentially >10×. | Convergence and tolerances; preconditioner may deteriorate with missingness; exact posterior variance is expensive. Secondary mean-only scope must not be substituted for the primary mean+variance endpoint. |
| 3 | Low-rank Nyström / inducing-point GP. | O(N r^2+r^3), storage O(Nr), versus O(N^3), O(N^2). At N=8192,r=256, leading fit ratio approximately N^2/(3r^2)≈341 before constants and prediction costs. | Approximate posterior; selecting landmarks or rank on test quality would leak. Identical quality not guaranteed. |
| 4 | Toeplitz/circulant FFT GP for stationary kernels on regular grids. | O(k N log N) iterative solves versus O(N^3); if true periodic covariance is diagonalized by FFT, O(N log N) direct solve. | Nonperiodic boundaries, log determinant and variances are not automatically exact. Circulant embedding changes the kernel unless proven otherwise. |
| 5 | Mixed-precision dense Cholesky plus double-precision iterative refinement. | Float32 Cholesky often ~2× throughput and half the factor memory; with factorization fraction f, S=1/((1-f)+f/2). Not expected to reach 10× on this CPU by itself. | GP matrices may be ill-conditioned; float32 can fail SPD checks. Refinement and full pipeline must be charged. |
| 6 | Batched / streamed posterior variance solves, cache blocking and controlled BLAS threads. | Same O(N^2 Q) arithmetic, fewer dispatches / allocations. If prediction occupies f and gains g, S=1/((1-f)+f/g). Expected 1.2–3×, not 10× overall. | Strong LAPACK baseline already does multiple RHS solves. Oversubscription can falsely inflate a baseline; thread settings must be equal. Useful baseline hygiene, not a principal claim. |
| 7 | Neural-network post-training low-rank factorization with calibration. | Replace d×d matrix with d×r and r×d: ideal component gain d/(2r). d=1024,r=32 gives 16×; overall if 80% compressible, S=1/(.2+.8/16)=4×. | Singular values do not guarantee task accuracy; calibration and decomposition costs; intentionally oversized baseline would be misleading. |
| 8 | INT8 neural inference with per-channel quantization. | ~4× lower weight bandwidth and possible SIMD gain. Full pipeline limited by unquantized activations, preprocessing and startup. 10× not reliable without specialized hardware. | Quality changes; hardware/library support absent; quantization setup must be included or explicitly amortized. |
| 9 | Exact prefix / embedding memoization for repeated requests. | S=1/((1-h)+h/c) for hit rate h and cache speed c. With c→infinity, need h≥.9 for 10×. | Would require an independently established repeated-request distribution, cache storage and invalidation; constructing repeats just for speed would violate the task's intent. |
| 10 | Speculative autoregressive decoding with exact accept/reject sampling. | If draft cost ε, accepted block length a, target verification cost v, S≈a/(v+ε). 10× needs large acceptance and batched verification. | No accelerator or pretrained language model; draft training and all rejected tokens count. No sensible validation within this sandbox. |

## Selection, before results

1. **Primary:** implement #1 as an exact modernization of a standard dense GP. Dense baseline uses optimized SciPy/LAPACK Cholesky and a multi-right-hand-side triangular solve, not Python loops or an explicit inverse. Both systems get the separable kernel components; the baseline is not forced to redundantly recompute pairwise distances.
2. **Secondary robustness study:** #2, only after the primary baseline and method are measured. Its endpoint and approximations will be labeled separately, never combined with the primary claim.
3. **Baseline/system audit:** #6, used to avoid a straw-man baseline and to test memory-versus-batching tradeoffs; any additional optimized baseline is diagnostic, while the primary baseline is frozen in the preregistration.

This selects two algorithmic ideas plus a measurement audit. #3–5 and #7–10 remain unimplemented unless explicitly logged otherwise. The synthetic gridded benchmark is chosen for a declared structural use case, not as evidence of general-purpose acceleration.
